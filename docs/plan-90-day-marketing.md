# Nautic Controls — 90-Day Marketing Plan

Prepared 2026-09-29. Built from a four-track research sweep (forums/Reddit, podcasts, YouTube, Facebook communities) on what works and what fails for new niche-hardware e-commerce stores. Full research: `research-niche-ecommerce-marketing-20260929.md` (companion file).

## Positioning (say this everywhere)

**"Marine and RV electronics, US stock."** Boards, kits, Signal K / NMEA 2000 gear. Ships from the US. Kit docs and a human who answers email.

Compete on availability, clear docs, and support - not on exclusive single-brand framing.

## Pricing recap (locked 2026-09-29)

- Engine kit: **$169.99** (~70% margin). $60–90 below finished WiFi gateways (iKonvert ~$260, ENGINELINK $236, YDWG-02 $249); $40 above the resistive-only KUS NKC42.
- Gateway kit: **$89.99**.
- Discounts: **warm-list events only** (holidays, campaign drops to the email list). Research is clear: discount tools don't move niche technical buyers — availability and clarity do. Never train buyers to wait for a sale.

## Phase 1 — Foundation (Days 1–30): earn the right to launch

**1. Enter 4–5 communities as a practitioner, not a vendor.**
Shortlist: OpenMarine forum, Signal K Discord + Google Group, Panbo forum, CruisersForum, YBW forums, Facebook "Boat Electrical and Electronic Q&A" (70k), "NMEA 2000 Support Group", "Raspberry Pi for Boats" (17k).
Rules: read pinned promo rules first; 2+ weeks of pure technical answers (NMEA 2000 wiring, Signal K questions) with zero business mention; profile clearly states what you do so the curious find it. Track group → DMs → deals in a spreadsheet; cut dead groups after 3 months. Buy the cheap forum sponsor slot where one exists instead of guerrilla posting.

**2. Publish the flagship build log.**
Full engine-kit install: old analog gauges → live phone dashboard, photos/video, honest limitations (what it doesn't do). Post it to OpenMarine, CruisersForum, and the store blog *before* pushing the store. This is the top of the funnel, the AI-search fodder (concrete specs and named use cases survive in AI answers; vague copy vanishes), and the trust proxy.

**3. Lead magnet + waitlist live.**
"NMEA 2000 refit checklist" PDF as the email capture. $10-deposit waitlist for the first restock — validates demand and seeds the launch list simultaneously. SMS on the same list, restock-alerts only.

**4. Seed 3–4 creators with long-term relationships.**
DIY sailing/tech builders (Low Cost Sailor tier), not gadget reviewers. Offer affiliate/rev-share, not one-off gifting — gifting blasts don't convert. Expect a months-long slow burn; get explicit opt-in mechanics for any borrowed audience from day one (never assume access to someone else's list).

**5. Film the install video (long-form) + cut Shorts from it.**
One flagship video; Shorts are the trailer for discovery, long-form is what sells a $90–170 considered purchase.

## Phase 2 — Launch (Days 31–45)

**6. One announcement per community, in the vendor/product-review area only.**
Use the accepted formula: plain hardware description + docs/video links + "happy to answer any technical questions." Proactively disambiguate: "Nautic Controls - not affiliated with Nauti-Control" (a similarly-named company is already active on CruisersForum).

**7. Store live, kits in stock, support inbox staffed.**
Budget the first two weeks overwhelmingly for support — answer within minutes, acknowledge issues openly, ship free fixes. Fast, honest support converts early buyers into evangelists. Do NOT run pre-orders as the buying experience; the 2–4-day-delivery promise is the differentiator, don't undermine it. Use the deposit waitlist only to size the next restock.

**8. Invite card in every kit box → users group.**
Discord (where the Signal K crowd already lives) or Facebook group, framed as support + install showcase, 90% peer help. Weekly rhythm: Monday question, Wednesday no-pitch tip, Friday member win.

---

## Phase 3 — Compound (Days 46–90)

**9. Weekly content rhythm, never coast.**
One install/education piece per week, repurposed across blog → video → community answers → email. A Google update once put a store 60% down YoY for coasting on stale content — keep pushing and diversify beyond Google.

**10. Email/SMS as the owned asset.**
Launches, restock alerts, new-guide notifications. First holiday/campaign discount goes to the warm list only. Target: email driving a real share of revenue within 6 months (comparable stores report 18–20%).

**11. Live Q&A cadence.**
Monthly live session (in/around the target groups): face on camera, real boat, real questions. Live video is the fastest trust vehicle, especially as AI-generated content makes text-only sellers look suspicious.

**12. Small capped paid-search test — only after organic compounds.**
"nmea 2000 wifi gateway", "engine monitor nmea 2000". Margins support it and competition is near-zero, but search volume is tiny: treat as an experiment, never the plan.

**13. Launch the "full refit bundle".**
Gateway + engine kit + cables at a slight discount. Raises average order value and makes the $169.99 engine kit feel like part of a system.

## Explicitly avoid

- Link-dropping in others' groups; bare "we launched" Reddit posts (half get removed, median 1 upvote).
- Catalog padding — the 2–3 kits ARE the store; don't dilute.
- Racing KUS/AliExpress on price — compete on docs, support, US stock.
- Building the only audience on Facebook/YouTube (rented land; Meta bans legitimate sellers with no recourse). Convert everything to the email list.
- Manufactured-controversy content; one-off influencer gifts; assuming access to anyone else's email list.
- Product Hunt-style launch directory blitzes — that audience doesn't buy boat hardware.
- Bot-looking posting cadence — mechanical-feeling promo about your own product reads as spam to mods and AI-paranoia filters alike.

## KPIs (review weekly)

- Email list size + waitlist deposits (leading indicator #1)
- Community answers posted → profile clicks → site visits
- Build-log / video views and watch time
- Kit sell-through rate and days-of-stock cover (never stock out silently — or if you do, say so loudly; scarcity honestly signaled is fine, surprise stockouts kill trust)
- Support response time (target: minutes in launch weeks)
- Repeat/referral rate from the users group

## Budget sketch

Near-zero paid spend in days 1–90. Money goes to: kit seed units for 3–4 creators, forum sponsor slots (cheap, hyper-targeted), basic video gear if needed, and the email/SMS tooling. The research is unambiguous: for niche hardware, a dozen paid-ad variants were tried and discarded by operators — content and community compound, ads don't.
